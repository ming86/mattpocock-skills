# Decide Under Uncertainty

Reach a defensible action under incomplete information by focusing on what actually separates the options, rather than filling out a pros-and-cons checklist.

Scale analysis to reversibility. When a choice is cheap to reverse, pick the leading option with a simple guardrail and learn from feedback. Reserve deep analysis for irreversible moves or ruinous downside.

Always compare against the real baseline (including doing nothing or staging commitment) using marginal future costs, switching friction, and opportunity cost rather than sunk costs. Screen non-negotiable constraints and catastrophic tail risk first; never let a weighted score average away a fatal flaw.

Compare surviving options in concrete units and trace likely second-order effects on systems, incentives, or future constraints. Separate irreducible noise (which calls for margin) from knowledge gaps: gather more information or run a small check only when different outcomes would change the action and the check costs less than the mistake it prevents. When estimates are disputed, find the switching threshold where the winning option flips instead of debating point probabilities.

Name the load-bearing assumption that would break the leading choice and, for high-uncertainty commitments, look for staged paths with clear exit or rollback triggers. Apply only the lenses that materially change the current decision; do not walk through every concept mechanically.
