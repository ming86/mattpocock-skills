# Readable Prose

Write for comprehension first.

Determine what needs to be communicated, how the ideas relate, and what the reader needs to understand. Let those relationships determine what to include, what deserves emphasis, and how the prose should be structured.

## Structure from the content

Organize the writing in the order that makes the subject easiest to understand, not necessarily the order in which the work was performed, discovered, or reasoned through.

Use causal order for causal explanations, dependency order for layered systems, chronological order when sequence itself matters, comparison when alternatives genuinely need comparison, and hierarchy when the material is hierarchical.

Supply the context the intended reader needs to understand what follows. Do not reproduce the author's entire path to understanding or explain background the reader can reasonably be expected to know.

Do not impose generic rhetorical structures when the content already has a natural structure.

Use headings, lists, tables, and other formatting only when they materially improve navigation or comprehension.

## Write directly and naturally

Prefer direct declarative sentences.

Use common words when they express the full meaning. Use technical or less common terms when they provide necessary precision or distinction.

Prefer conventional, idiomatic phrasing and established terminology. Do not invent labels, compressed compound terms, or abstract formulations when ordinary wording is clearer.

Match the register to the subject. Do not default to legal, governance, policy, bureaucratic, academic, or corporate language when the subject does not call for it.

Prefer concrete subjects and clear verbs where practical. Describe relationships by what actually happens rather than assigning abstract status when a direct verb or concrete rule states the meaning more clearly.

Avoid unnecessarily abstract, inflated, ornamental, or noun-heavy wording.

Keep terminology consistent unless a different term is needed for a meaningful distinction.

## Keep the signal-to-noise ratio high

Make each sentence contribute useful information or necessary connective reasoning.

Give more space to information that materially affects understanding: the main point, consequential reasoning, non-obvious relationships, important distinctions, and uncertainty that changes how the result should be interpreted. Compress routine background, incidental detail, obvious implications, and repeated support when further explanation adds little.

Preserve the reasoning chain under compression. Remove repetition and low-value detail before removing the relationships that explain why one point follows from another.

Remove filler, unnecessary scene-setting, generic transitions, and meta-commentary that merely announces importance, complexity, nuance, or what the prose is about to do.

State the substantive point directly when commentary about the point adds nothing.

Do not repeat the same proposition in slightly different words unless the repetition serves a clear explanatory purpose.

## Keep sentences and paragraphs readable

Keep the main subject and action easy to identify.

Avoid unnecessary nesting, long chains of modifiers, and sentences that make the reader hold several qualifications in mind before reaching the main point.

Break apart overloaded sentences when doing so improves comprehension.

Keep related sentences together in coherent paragraphs.

Do not default to one-sentence paragraphs or excessively fragmented prose.

Let sentence and paragraph length follow the ideas rather than a uniform pattern.

Develop an idea enough for the reader to understand the mechanism, reasoning, or implication when that detail matters.

## Avoid formulaic rhetoric

Do not manufacture contrasts, reframings, pivots, revelations, or dramatic emphasis merely to make the prose sound polished.

Avoid habitual constructions that turn a simple point into a rhetorical progression.

Do not describe a point as important, interesting, revealing, nuanced, or worth considering when the prose can simply communicate what makes it so.

Let emphasis come from the importance and placement of the idea rather than ornamental phrasing.

## Preserve meaning and precision

Readability does not mean removing necessary complexity.

Preserve important qualifications, constraints, causal relationships, uncertainty, and technical distinctions.

For analytical or explanatory writing, use concrete examples, observations, measurements, or other support when they materially help the reader understand why an important claim or conclusion follows. Do not add support ceremonially or merely because it is available.

When revising existing text, do not alter the author's intended meaning, stance, or level of certainty merely to make the prose smoother.

Match vocabulary, assumed knowledge, and explanatory depth to the intended reader, purpose, and subject, and readily understandable to an international audience.
