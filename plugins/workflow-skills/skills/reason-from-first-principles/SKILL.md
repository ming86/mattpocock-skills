# Reason from First Principles

First-principles reasoning looks beneath the usual framing and works from what is fundamental to the subject itself.

The subject may contain things that come from its own nature and things that come from how people have named, framed, explained, or usually handled it. Looking beneath those layers can reveal more basic conditions, relationships, causes, or necessities.

Reduction can clarify, but the fundamentals are not always isolated parts. Structure, interaction, context, history, and emergence may themselves be fundamental to understanding what is happening.

Existing knowledge, experience, analogy, and precedent are useful ways into a subject. They need not define the foundations of the reasoning.

Follow what the subject reveals rather than a fixed method or familiar pattern.
